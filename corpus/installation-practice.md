# Field Installation Practice

## Enclosure Mounting

Splitter enclosures should be mounted at a height that keeps them clear of expected flood levels and away from routine foot or vehicle traffic. A pole-mounted enclosure is generally easier to service than a pedestal-mounted one but is more exposed to wind loading, so hardware should be rated accordingly. Labeling the enclosure with its identifier at the time of mounting saves a return visit later when the same box needs to be located from a work order.

## Fusion Splicing Practice

A clean, well-aligned fusion splice should introduce only a small, consistent loss, and a batch of splices on the same job should read close to each other — a single outlier splice usually means a cleave defect or contamination rather than a systematic problem with the technique. Splice loss should be measured and logged at the time of installation rather than assumed from a nominal figure, because it is the one loss source most sensitive to workmanship on the day.

## Connector Handling and Cleaning

Every connector should be inspected and cleaned immediately before mating, even a connector that appears new out of its packaging — dust accumulates during shipping and storage, and a contaminated connector is one of the most common causes of an installation that measures worse than its design budget predicted. A fiber inspection scope is inexpensive relative to the cost of a return visit and should be treated as standard kit, not an optional extra.
